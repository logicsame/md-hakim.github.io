I am an AI researcher working on **reasoning efficiency in large language models**: how the way a model is trained decides how well it spends compute at inference time. I am the **sole author of a NeurIPS 2026 main-track paper**, which shows across 50 models that training quality, more than parameter count or token budget, sets when extra reasoning stops paying off. My current work follows three threads:

<ol class="threads">
  <li><strong>Training quality and test-time reasoning.</strong> When do extra reasoning tokens help, waste compute, or hurt accuracy, and which training choices move that boundary.</li>
  <li><strong>Reinforcement learning for reasoning.</strong> Reward signals for RL post-training that teach reasoning models to stop once they have the answer, instead of over-generating.</li>
  <li><strong>Looped language models.</strong> Models that reuse the same layers several times: whether a model should think deeper or longer, and how it can choose its own number of loops per input.</li>
</ol>

Earlier, I built bio-inspired, self-repairing neural networks and studied how intrusion detectors fail across industrial networks, which led to **two first-author papers in Scientific Reports** (Nature Portfolio) and **three at IEEE QPAIN 2025**. I am also a **Kaggle Notebooks Master**.
