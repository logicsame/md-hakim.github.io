<p>I am an AI researcher working on <strong>reasoning efficiency in large language models</strong>: how the way a model is trained decides how well it spends compute at inference time. I am the <strong>sole author of a NeurIPS 2026 main-track paper</strong>, and my work follows <a href="#publications">three connected threads</a>:</p>

<ol class="threads">
  <li><strong>Training quality and test-time reasoning:</strong> Measuring when extra reasoning tokens help, when they waste compute, and when they hurt accuracy. Across 50 models from 0.5B to 685B parameters, I showed that training methodology, more than parameter count or token budget, sets these efficiency boundaries.</li>
  <li><strong>Reinforcement learning for reasoning:</strong> Designing reward signals for RL post-training that teach reasoning models to stop once they have reached the answer, instead of over-generating until they run out of budget.</li>
  <li><strong>Looped language models:</strong> Studying models that reuse the same layers several times: whether a model should think deeper or longer, and how it can choose its own number of loops for each input.</li>
</ol>

<p>Earlier, I worked on bio-inspired, self-repairing neural networks and on the robustness of intrusion detection across industrial networks. This produced <strong>two first-author papers in Scientific Reports (Nature Portfolio)</strong> and <strong>three in IEEE QPAIN 2025</strong>. I review for <strong>Frontiers in AI, Computational Neuroscience, and Nutrition</strong>, and I am a <a href="https://www.kaggle.com/hakim11"><strong>Kaggle Notebooks Master</strong></a>.</p>
