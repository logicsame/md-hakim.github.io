I work on **reasoning efficiency in large language models**: how the way a model is trained decides how well it spends compute at inference time. My **sole-authored NeurIPS 2026 main-track paper** shows that training quality, not parameter count or token budget, sets when extra reasoning stops paying off. I now follow that question along three connected threads.

Before this, I built bio-inspired, self-repairing neural networks and studied how intrusion detectors fail across industrial networks, work that led to **two first-author papers in Scientific Reports** (Nature Portfolio) and **three at IEEE QPAIN 2025**.
